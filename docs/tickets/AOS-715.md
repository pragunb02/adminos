## Description
When a subscription's charge rises beyond a tolerance, tell the user once per new price, with the old and new amount, through the existing notification service. The same rule decides the waste score's price-rise signal.

## Why
Research report §12: `priceChanged` compares only the last two charges (a GST rounding change or one odd charge is noise), and there is no alert at the moment of the rise.

## Scope
- **In:** a pure price-change rule; rise records (idempotent) and their notification; the detector's `priceChanged` / `priceChangePct` from the same rule (feeds D-58).
- **Out:** a web change (the Subscriptions page already shows ↑/↓ %); alerts for price cuts; a same-day trigger on each new transaction (see Design).

## Acceptance criteria
- [x] A rise is the latest charge at least max(5%, ₹20) above the **median of the up-to-3 charges before it**, compared in whole rupees (so ₹648.99 → ₹649.00 isn't a change). → `PriceChangeRuleTest`
- [x] Annual plans: a yearly or quarterly subscription is judged against its own earlier charges (one is enough); a charge 8–14× the usual (monthly → yearly plan) or 1/14–1/8 of it (yearly → monthly) is a plan switch, not a price change. Monthly and weekly plans need 2 earlier charges. → `PriceChangeRuleTest`, `PriceRiseAlertsTest`
- [x] Charges in another currency than the latest aren't compared. → `PriceChangeRuleTest`
- [x] One notification per (subscription, new price): re-running detection, the same price next month, or two runs at once never notify twice; a failed send is retried on the next run; a user who switched subscription alerts off isn't told later either. → `PriceRiseAlertsTest`, `PriceRiseIntegrationTest` (unique key, 5 concurrent records → 1)
- [x] The notification says the old and new amount and the change: "Netflix went up: ₹649 → ₹799 (+23%)", body "Charged ₹799 on 11 Oct 2026; it was usually ₹649. If it isn't worth the new price, Subscriptions shows how to cancel.", and opens Subscriptions. → `PriceRiseAlertsTest`
- [x] `priceChanged` / `priceChangePct` (and so the waste score's price-rise factor) come from the same rule (a +₹16 change no longer scores; a rise does). → `PriceRiseAlertsTest`

## Design
- **Context:**
  - `SubscriptionDetectorService` groups a user's spends by merchant and cycle every night (04:00, D-50) and sets `priceChanged` when the last two charges differ by more than 5%.
  - `WasteScorer` (D-58) gives 30 points (40 at ≥20%) for `priceChanged` with a positive `priceChangePct`.
  - `NotificationService` delivers by the user's preferences (`subscriptionFlags`) and quiet hours (D-51).
- **Options considered:**
  1. **Detect in the nightly detection run, record the rise in its own table with a unique (subscription, price) key, then notify the unnotified ones** (chosen). Detection already has the grouped charges; the unique key makes "once" a database fact; a failed send stays unnotified and is retried the next night.
  2. **A post-commit `TransactionCreatedListener`** (D-53's hook) that re-groups the merchant's charges on every new spend. Same-day alerts, but it duplicates the detector's grouping (and AOS-709's aliases) in a second place, and runs inside ingestion.
  3. **A flag on the subscription row** ("notified for price X"). No new table, but it keeps only the last rise, and a rise → drop → rise to the same price is indistinguishable from a repeat.
- **Decision:** option 1 (D-74). **Patterns:** Specification (`PriceChangeRule`, pure), Repository (`PriceRiseRepository`), idempotency by a unique constraint, Template (detect → record → notify) in the job.
- **Extensibility:** a price-cut alert or an in-app "price history" is a read of the same table; another channel is a D-51 channel.
- **Impact:**
  - **Domain:** `PriceChangeRule` + `PriceRise` (financial), detector uses them; `PriceRiseAlerts` (notifications) sends; `SubscriptionDetectionJob` runs detect then notify per user.
  - **Schema:** `V34__subscription_price_rises.sql` (tentative number).
  - **API/Web:** unchanged.

## Risks / notes
- **When the alert arrives:** the night after the charge (detection runs at 04:00), held by quiet hours like other non-critical notices (D-51). A same-day alert would be the listener option; not worth a second grouping today.
- **Delivery is at least once:** if marking a rise as told fails after the send, it is sent again the next night (D-51: a duplicate beats a lost notice).
- **Plan switches are guessed from the ratio** (8–14×). A real 8–14× price rise would be missed; a yearly plan at a steep discount (under 8×) alerts as a rise. Both are rare and the waste score still shows the change.
- **Pre-existing, not fixed here (reported):** after a monthly → yearly plan switch the detector keeps the monthly cycle, projects the next charge a month later and, two months on, marks the subscription ended until the next yearly charge. It belongs to the detector's cycle logic (a follow-up).

## Proposed decision D-74
> **D-74 — A subscription price rise is told once per new price, by the rule the waste score uses** · Type: **Product** · Tasks: AOS-715 · 2026-10-17 · Builds on D-51, D-58, D-65
>
> **Context:** `priceChanged` compared only the last two charges (GST rounding or one odd charge was noise), and nothing told the user when a price went up.
>
> **Decision:**
> - **The rule (`PriceChangeRule`):** the latest charge vs the median of the up-to-3 charges before it, in whole currency units, same currency only; a change is at least max(5%, ₹20); 8–14× (or 1/14–1/8) is a monthly ↔ yearly plan switch, not a change; monthly and weekly plans need 2 earlier charges, yearly and quarterly 1.
> - **One rule for three things:** the alert, the Subscriptions page's ↑/↓ (`priceChanged`, `priceChangePct`) and the waste score's price-rise factor (D-58).
> - **Once per new price:** the nightly detection records each rise in `subscription_price_rises` (V34), unique per (subscription, new price in whole units); the same job then sends the untold ones as a subscription alert ("Netflix went up: ₹649 → ₹799 (+23%)", opening Subscriptions) and marks them told. A failed send is retried the next night; a user with subscription alerts off is not told later.
> - **Price cuts are shown, not alerted.**
>
> **Alternatives rejected:** a per-transaction listener (a second copy of the grouping, inside ingestion), a "notified price" flag on the subscription (forgets history; can't tell a rise back to an old price from a repeat), last-two comparison (noisy).
>
> **Consequences:** a price history per subscription exists for later UI; the thresholds are constants to tune on real data.
