## Description
The owner sometimes buys with pay-later (LazyPay, Simpl, Amazon Pay Later, Flipkart Pay Later and similar). That spending happens with no bank record, and the debt is easy to forget until a bill is due.

This task:
- turns pay-later **bill and due messages** (SMS and email) into bills in the dues calendar, with the usual reminders (AOS-503);
- lets a **bank repayment** mark the bill paid (D-54) without counting it as spending twice;
- **tags pay-later purchases** ("₹450 spent at Swiggy using LazyPay");
- serves **pay-later debt outstanding** per provider for the coach;
- shows in **Review** how much of a bill has no recorded purchases.

## Why
- BRD §5.6 and AOS-S9.4: "A pay-later bill appears in dues with its date and amount".
- D-80: the pay-later habit lens needs to know what is owed.
- Before this task:
  - a provider's "your bill is due" SMS was dropped as an "upcoming payment notice";
  - a repayment to the provider counted as a new "Other" spend.

## Scope
- **In:**
  - providers as data;
  - provider SMS (bill, purchase, repayment confirmation);
  - provider emails (bill, purchase);
  - `pay_later` as a bill type and a payment method;
  - repayments as transfers that pay the bill;
  - `GET /api/v1/pay-later`;
  - the Review gap note.
- **Out:**
  - paying through providers (the coach informs only, D-80);
  - the coach screen (E9-A);
  - credit limits, interest and late fees;
  - provider refunds;
  - pay-later orders seen only in receipts (AOS-903 marks them `no_payment`; they are not transactions).

## Acceptance criteria
- [x] A pay-later bill SMS or email becomes a bill: type `pay_later`, the provider as biller, the amount, the minimum due when shown, and the due date. It shows in `GET /api/v1/dues` (detail `pay_later`) and gets the bill reminders.
  - A due date without a year takes the next sensible year.
  - Repeated reminders, and the same bill by SMS and email, are one bill.
  - A bill without a readable due date is skipped and counted.
  (`PayLaterSmsParserTest`, `PayLaterFlowTest`, `TestPayLaterEmailsBecomeBillsAndTaggedPurchases`, `TestGmailPersistPayLater_Integration`)
- [x] A provider's purchase message becomes a spend at the merchant (categorised as usual), paid with `pay_later`, tagged with the provider in `metadata.payLater`. This holds for SMS and for email. (`PayLaterFlowTest`, Go unit and integration tests, `PayLaterIntegrationTest`)
- [x] A provider's "payment received" message is not a transaction. (`PayLaterSmsParserTest`, Go `TestPayLaterKinds`)
- [x] A bank debit to a pay-later provider is a transfer (`pay_later_bill`), like a card bill (D-39). It marks the provider's open bill paid, in both directions: when the payment arrives, and on the sweep when the bill arrives after the repayment. (`PayLaterFlowTest`, `PayLaterIntegrationTest` on Postgres)
- [x] A bank's own alert that names a provider ("debited from A/c XX1234 to LAZYPAY") is parsed as a bank debit. A mandate notice about a provider goes to the mandate parser. (`PayLaterSmsParserTest`)
- [x] `GET /api/v1/pay-later` returns, per provider:
  - outstanding = unpaid bills + purchases since the latest bill;
  - the unpaid bills, the unbilled purchases (with transaction IDs), the next due date and purchases in the last 30 days;
  - the **gaps**;
  - and the total.
  Only the user's own data is used. (`PayLaterFlowTest`, `PayLaterRoutesTest`)
- [x] **Review** shows, for each recent bill larger than the purchases recorded for it: "LazyPay bill ₹1,200 (due 3 Nov): only ₹200 of purchases recorded, so ₹1,000 isn't in your spending". (`PayLaterGaps.test.tsx`, `e2e/pay-later.spec.ts`)

## Design
- **Context:**
  - SMS are parsed in the API (D-33). Gmail is parsed in the Go worker, which writes bills directly (AOS-503).
  - Bill matching follows D-54. Card bills are transfers (D-39).
  - A pay-later provider behaves like a card: purchases first, then one repayment.
- **Options considered (how pay-later is counted):**
  1. **Card model** — **chosen.** Purchases are spends at the merchant, on the day, with their category; the repayment is a transfer that pays the bill. Mirrors D-39, keeps the right category and day, and never double counts.
  2. **Cash model:** purchases are informational and the repayment is the spend. Totals are always complete, but the category is lost and the spend lands weeks late.
  3. **A separate debt ledger:** a second source of truth next to transactions and bills.
- **The risk of option 1, settled:** a purchase that left no message would be missing from spending. It is made **visible, not silently corrected**, as the coordinator asked:
  - each recent bill is compared with the purchases recorded since the provider's previous bill (or the 31 days before);
  - the difference is `gaps[].unrecorded` in `GET /api/v1/pay-later` and a note in **Review**;
  - nothing is invented: the app doesn't know what that money bought.
- **Options considered (where provider SMS are read):**
  1. **A pay-later step in the SMS registry, before the drop rules**, only when the text names a provider and isn't a bank account alert (masked account digits) or a mandate notice — **chosen**.
  2. Sender headers only: provider headers vary and aren't public.
- **Patterns:**
  - Strategy (`PayLaterSmsParser`; Go `processPayLater`);
  - data-driven providers (`PayLaterProviders`; Go `payLaterProviders`);
  - Specification (`TransferDetector.isPayLaterRepayment`);
  - D-54's matcher, extended with one transfer subcategory.
- **Extensibility:** a new provider is one entry on each side, plus its catalogue aliases.
- **Impact:**
  - DB: `V51__pay_later.sql`.
  - API:
    - `PaymentMethod.PAY_LATER`, `BillType.PAY_LATER`;
    - `domain/financial/paylater` (providers, `PayLaterBills`, `PayLaterService`, routes);
    - `PayLaterSmsParser`, the registry, `SmsIntake` (new `Bill` result), `RawSmsRoutes`, the WhatsApp import;
    - `ToTransactionStep` (carries `payLater`), `TransferDetector`, `TransferStep`, `BillPaymentMatcher`;
    - catalogue (7 brands).
  - Go:
    - `handlers/gmail_paylater.go`, provider emails handled before receipts;
    - `ingest_events` insert carries `payment_method` and `metadata`;
    - bill insert skips a pay-later bill already known for that provider and due date;
    - due dates like `03-Nov-2026`;
    - filters (`lazypay.in`, `getsimpl.com`).
  - Web: `PayLaterGaps` in Review; test and e2e defaults.

## Security (D-100, ENGINEERING §4a)
- **What is exposed:**
  - one new authenticated GET (`/api/v1/pay-later`);
  - new input: provider SMS and emails;
  - new stored data: bills and transactions of existing kinds;
  - one new web section.
  - No new dependency, listener or outbound call.

| Threat | What stops it |
|---|---|
| **A forged provider SMS** from a personal number | `SenderId` refuses phone numbers. Promotional senders are dropped. OTP texts are dropped before anything else. (`PayLaterSmsParserTest`) |
| A text mimicking a provider to **turn a real bank debit into a "transfer"** | A transfer needs the *payment's own narration or UPI ID* to name a provider (whole words: "SIMPLY FRESH" is not Simpl). A bank alert is never read as a provider message. Repayment transfers are visible in the feed. |
| **A forged provider email** | The provider is decided by the sender's address domain, never the display name. Look-alike domains are rejected. Amazon, Flipkart and Paytm also need the provider's name *in the subject*, so a receipt "paid via Flipkart Pay Later" stays a receipt. (Go tests) |
| Hostile content: **unicode tricks** | A hidden character in a provider's name means it isn't one. Look-alike digits are never an amount. |
| Hostile content: **implausible numbers and dates** | Amounts over ₹10 lakh are refused. A minimum due above the bill is dropped. Impossible dates (31 Feb) and dates more than a year away are refused. |
| Hostile content: **oversized texts** | Texts over 2,000 characters are not provider notices. |
| **Untrusted text in the web** | Provider names render as React text. A unit test and an e2e test check that `<img onerror>` stays text. |
| **Raw text** reaching storage or logs (D-33) | Only structured fields are kept. Logs carry IDs and dates only. |
| **Another user's data** | Every query is scoped to the signed-in user (`PayLaterFlowTest`). Signed out, or a token that can't be refreshed, ends at sign-in (route test, e2e). |
| **Request bodies** (D-92) | The new route has no body. The AOS-903 mutation tests now send the app's `Origin`, run with the body cap, and test a 413. |

## Proposed decision D-89
### D-89 — Pay-later is counted like a card; gaps are shown, not guessed
2026-10-18 · Proposed (AOS-909, background agent) · Type: Product / Architecture · Tasks: AOS-909, AOS-901, AOS-902 · Builds on D-39, D-54, D-55, D-59
- **Counting:**
  - A pay-later purchase is a spend at the merchant, with `paymentMethod = pay_later` and `metadata.payLater = <provider>`.
  - The bank repayment to the provider is a **transfer** (`subcategory = pay_later_bill`), as card bills are (D-39), and it pays the provider's bill (D-54, both directions).
- **Bills:**
  - A provider's bill (SMS or email) is a bill of type `pay_later` with the provider as biller, so dues and reminders apply (D-55).
  - The same provider and due date (or within 2 days with the same amount) is one bill, across reminders and channels.
- **Providers are data** on both sides: LazyPay, Simpl, Amazon Pay Later, Flipkart Pay Later, ICICI PayLater, Paytm Postpaid, MobiKwik ZIP.
  - Provider SMS are read before the drop rules, and only when the text names a provider and isn't a bank account alert or a mandate notice.
  - Provider email is decided by the sender's domain; shared domains (Amazon, Flipkart, Paytm) also need the provider's name in the subject.
- **The gap is visible:** a bill larger than the purchases recorded for its period shows the difference in `GET /api/v1/pay-later` and in Review. It is never filled in by guessing.
- **Debt outstanding** = unpaid pay-later bills + pay-later purchases since the provider's latest bill.
- **Rejected:**
  - the cash model (category and timing lost);
  - a separate debt ledger (a second source of truth);
  - recognising provider SMS by sender header only (headers vary and aren't public).
