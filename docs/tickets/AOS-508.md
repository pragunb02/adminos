## Description
`BillTrackingService.matchPayment` existed but nothing called it. It also could never match a credit-card bill, because card-bill payments are transfers (AOS-208), not spends.

Now:
- every stored transaction is matched against the user's open bills;
- a daily sweep pays bills whose payment was recorded before the bill was detected.

So nobody is reminded of a bill they've already paid.

## Acceptance criteria
- [x] **What can pay a bill:** spends and credit-card bill transfers. Self-transfers, cash withdrawals, refunds and income never do.
- [x] **Amount:** within 5% of the bill, or the minimum due (±1%). The coverage (`full` / `minimum_due`) is recorded on the bill. A ₹0 bill needs no payment.
- [x] **Window** (Indian dates):
  - from the billing period's end (or 40 days before the due date) to 15 days after it, so late payments count;
  - a billing period ending after the due date is ignored.
- [x] **Evidence it's this biller,** strongest first:
  - the bill's account/card last 4 in the payment, matched as whole digits (45210 isn't 4521);
  - the payee's name similar to the biller's;
  - for a card-bill payment, it being the only credit-card bill that fits.
- [x] **Ranking:** stronger evidence first, then the closer amount, then the nearer due date. A true tie marks nothing paid: no guessing.
- [x] **Which bills:** paid, cancelled and already-linked bills, and other users' bills, are never matched. An overdue bill can still be paid late.
- [x] **Idempotent:** matching the same payment twice changes nothing.
- [x] **Daily sweep:** in `bill-reminders`, before reminders are sent, upcoming and overdue bills are matched against payments in their window (counted as `paidBySweep`).
- [x] **Postgres keeps bill metadata on update.** Before the fix, the in-memory repository kept it and Postgres dropped it.

## Design
- **Options considered:**
  1. **A pure `BillPaymentMatcher`** (rules, ranking, ambiguity) used by a post-commit listener (AOS-505's hook) and the daily sweep (chosen).
  2. **Keep the legacy name+amount check:** no window, no card bills, no tie handling, and the first bill wins.
  3. **Link bills to payments through the Review screen:** a more manual step for something the rules can decide.
- **Decision:** option 1 (D-54). **Patterns:**
  - Observer (`BillPaymentListener`);
  - Specification-style evidence ranking;
  - the same matcher in both directions (`billFor` a payment, `paymentFor` a bill).
- **Extensibility:** a new kind of evidence (e.g. a biller ID such as a BBPS consumer number) is one `BillEvidence` value plus one check in `evidence()`.
- **Impact:**
  - `BillPaymentMatcher`;
  - `BillTrackingService.matchPayment`, rewritten, plus `matchEarlierPayments`;
  - `BillPaymentListener`;
  - `BillRemindersJob`, which now runs the sweep first;
  - `ExposedBillRepository.update`, which now writes metadata.

## Verification
- `./gradlew test integrationTest koverVerify`: 1,177 unit and 104 integration tests, 0 disabled, 99.4% lines.
- Matcher tests cover:
  - what can pay a bill;
  - full and minimum-due amounts;
  - the window edges, the billing-period start and Indian dates;
  - each kind of evidence, including whole-digit card numbers and the "only card bill" rule;
  - excluded bills;
  - ranking and ties;
  - the sweep direction;
  - service idempotency;
  - the listener.
- Postgres end to end:
  - a "CC PAYMENT XX4521 BILLDESK" SMS becomes a transfer and pays the HDFC card bill by account number;
  - the sweep pays an Airtel bill whose payment came first.

## Risks / notes
- **Found while testing:** `ExposedBillRepository.update` never wrote `metadata`, so in-memory and Postgres behaved differently. Fixed here, because this task records its match details there. The regression is caught by the Postgres end-to-end test.
- **Partial card payments:** a payment between the minimum and the full amount doesn't match. Many users pay a round partial amount; a "partially paid" status is a possible later step.
- **Unchanged:** bill dates are Indian dates (bills come from Indian billers); custom reminder days are still not persisted (AOS-503).
