## Description
Money from the bank to investments (SIPs, lump sums, broker top-ups, NPS, PPF, FDs) and back (redemptions, payouts, FD maturities) gets a new nature, `INVESTMENT`: a kind of transfer, so it is neither spent nor received. A pipeline step after `TransferStep` sets it from a data list of payee patterns; dividends stay income. A read-time matcher links each bank flow to the ledger row it paid for (purchase + stamp duty, to the paisa) or was paid by (a sale), and lists the unmatched and ambiguous ones for Review.

## Why
Today a ₹10,000 SIP debit counts as spending while the same money is a purchase in the ledger, which inflates spend and understates savings (WEALTH-RESEARCH §4.3, D-79).

**Coordination with AOS-908 (savings rate, PR #111, D-87):** 908 defines `rate = (income − spent) ÷ income` from the console's analytics, with income = `INCOME` nature and spent = `SPEND − REFUND`. Analytics count only `spend`, `refund` and `income` (an allowlist, `Analytics.COUNTED` and the SQL in `ExposedAnalyticsRepository`), so `INVESTMENT` rows are excluded with no change to 908: a SIP counts as **saved**, and a redemption credit is **not income**, so moving money out of a fund can't inflate the rate. Test: `a SIP debit is not spending and a redemption is not income` (in memory) and `InvestmentNatureIntegrationTest` (Postgres analytics).

## Scope
- **In:** `TransactionNature.INVESTMENT` + V73; `InvestmentFlowRules` (`resources/wealth/investment-flows.json`), `InvestmentFlowStep` in the pipeline; `InvestmentFlowService` (matching on read) and `GET /wealth/investment-flows`; a nature golden set; the categorisation labeller mirrors the new step.

## Design
- **Options considered:**
  1. **Where the nature is decided:** (a) a category "investment" (a new category enum value, DB enum, web, AI prompts and every golden baseline would change); (b) **a nature** set by a pipeline step, leaving categories alone. D-39 makes nature drive totals; (b) is the smaller, truer change. Chosen (b).
  2. **Recognition:** (a) hard-coded regexes in Kotlin; (b) **a data file** of payees and patterns (D-44 style), validated at start-up. Chosen (b).
  3. **Matching:** (a) a stored link table written at import and ingest; (b) **computed on read** from the bank rows and the ledger. (b) is always consistent with undo and late imports, and the data is small. Chosen (b).
  4. **Golden set:** (a) add rows to the D-61 categorisation file (re-records every baseline and AI record, some needing the owner's local models); (b) **a separate nature golden file** plus a gate that the D-61 file's investment-tagged rows (and only those) become `INVESTMENT`. Chosen (b).
- **Decision:** proposed **D-165** below. **Patterns:** pipeline step (Chain of Responsibility, D-34), data-driven rules, pure matcher.
- **Extensibility:** a new platform is one pattern in the JSON; a new matching rule (e.g. mandate-backed SIPs, D-56) is a branch in `fits`.
- **Impact:** a new enum value (Kotlin + V73); every `when (nature)` compiles (one test fixture updated); `PipelineLabeller` gains the step; one AI eval record's metrics.

## Security (D-100, docs/ENGINEERING.md §4a)
- **Exposes:** `GET /api/v1/wealth/investment-flows` (read-only); a pipeline step over bank text (already untrusted input); a data file loaded at start-up.
- **Abuse and what stops it:**
  - *Stranger / forged token:* inside `authenticate("auth-jwt")` (route test 401; route inventory).
  - *Another user's data:* both reads are scoped by the token's user (`findByUserId`, `ledger.active(userId)`); IdorTest list row; route test shows nothing of the owner's to another user.
  - *Hostile narration (T4, a crafted SMS/email):* regexes run on at most 1,000 characters with simple patterns (no nested quantifiers); the worst a forged narration can do is mark a debit as an investment (no money moves, nothing is sent); the owner sees it in Review as unmatched. Merchant text is returned as data (rendered as text in the web).
  - *Range abuse:* at most 3 years and 2,000 rows per report.
  - *AI exposure:* investment rows are now excluded from the AI fallback (it only takes spend/refunds), which sends less data, not more.
- **Data at rest:** one more enum value; nothing new stored. **Dependencies:** none.

## Proposed decision D-165 (for the integrator)
### D-165 — `INVESTMENT` nature from a payee data list; matched to the ledger on read; the savings rate counts it as saved
2026-10-18 · Proposed · Type: Business logic · Tasks: AOS-815 · Builds on D-39, D-44, D-46, D-61, D-79, D-87
- A pipeline step after `TransferStep` marks debits and credits whose text names a fund house, MF platform, clearing corporation, RTA, broker, NPS, PPF or FD (`resources/wealth/investment-flows.json`) as nature `investment`; dividend/IDCW credits stay income; transfers, refunds, reversals and owner-set categories are untouched; categories are not changed.
- `investment` is excluded from spent and received everywhere (analytics allowlist), so the savings rate (income − spent) ÷ income counts SIPs as saved and redemptions as not income.
- A bank debit matches a ledger purchase when it equals purchase + stamp duty to the paisa, purchase dated T−1…T+4; a credit matches a sale of the same amount T−5…T; one-to-one; ties match nothing; unmatched and ambiguous flows are shown in Review. Computed on read.
- Measured by a nature golden set and a gate on the D-61 set's investment-tagged rows. LRS remittances are out until the owner decides how to tell them apart.
- **Rejected:** a new "investment" category; hard-coded patterns; a stored link table; growing the D-61 categorisation file (re-records every baseline and AI record).
